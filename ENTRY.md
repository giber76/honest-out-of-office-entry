# The reply (179 characters, one sentence)

> I'm out of office until Monday, and honestly your email will join the 300 unread ones I'm pretending don't exist — I'll face them once I've recovered, thank you for understanding.

## Why this line wins on the judge's brief

1. **Honest and polite, both.** It confesses something real — the sender is
   pretending 300 unread emails don't exist — and still closes warmly, so it
   survives being forwarded to a manager. Neither rude nor merely cheerful.
2. **Admits something only said privately.** The inbox is not "being
   monitored"; it is being avoided. The sender will face it only "once I've
   recovered" — recovery, not diligence, is the plan.
3. **Warm, professional tone.** "Honestly" softens the confession, the
   recovery promise is human, and the thank-you keeps the sender employed.
4. **One sentence, 179 characters.** No cramming, no trailing off, one period
   at the very end.
5. **Quotable turn of phrase.** "Pretending don't exist" and "face them once
   I've recovered" — the second read is the honest one: the sender is not
   behind on email, they are hiding from it. Chosen over seven other drafts:
   the avoidance confession beats the inbox-futility line on honesty-first,
   the judge's top weight, while the warm close keeps it polite.
